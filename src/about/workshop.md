For the first 30 years of my life, everything I learned about woodworking came from working with my father in his workshop. As mentioned elsewhere here, my father used funds he earned while in the US Army in Korea to build a small, but capable workshop. Like me, he had to stuff all his tools in a small space (in his case a basement room, in my case an external garage). Later, when he built a workbench for my brother and me, he had to give up some of that limited space for us. 

When you look at my current shop, you'll see a lot of things that came directly from my father's.

The following photo is of the corner of my dad's workshop, with his workbench and table saw in prominence. I still have the workbench, it's underneath the pegboard in the header photo on this page.

![An photo of my father's workbench](/images/workshop/workshop-v1-1.jpg)
{% imageCaption "workshop-v1-1" "Workbench and Table Saw" %}

If you look at the upper-left corner of the image, you'll see that he had a stack of hammers hanging across the top of the pegboard. When I moved in to my current shop, the first thing I built after I mounted the same pegboard to the wall was the hammer rack you see at the top of this page's header image.

In the middle-right of the image, you'll see a rack containing a bunch of baby food jars containing screws, bolts, and other weird stuff my dad wanted to keep handy. I still have every one of those jars, stored on a wooden shelf as shown in the following photo.

![Updated Jar Storage](/images/workshop/workshop-v2-01.jpg)
{% imageCaption "workshop-v1-2" "Updated Jar Storage" %}

On one wall of the shop, my dad used stacks of cigar boxes to store a whole variety of things; you can see them on the right of the following figure. Things like felt pads, equipment parts, bolts, screw, and more. 

![Another photo of the workshop highlighting the cigar box wall](/images/workshop/workshop-v1-2.jpg)
{% imageCaption "workshop-v1-2" "Cigar Box Storage" %}

I duplicated the same setup in my shop, replacing a lot of the cardboard cigar boxes with wooden ones I purchased in bulk on ebay. That was a great find, I wish I could get more.

![a photo of the updated Cigar Box Storage](/images//workshop/workshop-v2-02.jpg)
{% imageCaption "workshop-v2-02" "Updated Cigar Box Storage" %}

On the left side of {% imageReference "workshop-v1-2" %} are a bunch of Rubbermaid tubs with lids. The tubs were so old that if you touched them they'd fall apart. So, before I moved the shop to North Carolina, I replaced the tubs with really crappy pine drawers I'd built in a hurry. Now, 16 years later, I've started replacing the pine drawers with poplar ones I built with much higher quality using different construction methods. As you can see from the following image, I've replaced the top set with new ones, and now I have to build the rest of them.

![a photo of Wooden storage drawers](/images//workshop/workshop-v2-03.jpg)
{% imageCaption "workshop-v2-03" "Wooden storage drawers" %}

As you can see, he had to stuff tools, books, and other stuff into all nooks and crannies in the shop.

![A photo of my Father's table saw](/images//workshop/workshop-v1-3.jpg)
{% imageCaption "workshop-v1-3" "The Back Corner" %}

Tucked in a back corner (behind the house's water heater) was a lathe. That is the only tool of my father's that I never used, I sold it a few years back.
