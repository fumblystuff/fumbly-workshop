For the first 30 years of my life, everything I learned about woodworking came from working with my father in his workshop. As mentioned elsewhere, my father used funds he earned while in the US Army in Korea to build a small, but capable workshop. Like me, he had to stuff all his tools in a small space (in his case a basement room, in my case an external garage). When he built a workbench for my brother and me, he had to give up some of that limited space for us. When you look at my shop, you'll see a lot of things that came directly from my father's shop.

The following image is of the corner of my dad's workshop, with his workbench and table saw in prominence. I still have the workbench, it's underneath the pegboard in the header image on this page.

![An image of my father's workbench](/images/workshop-v1-1.jpg)
{% imageCaption "workshop-v1-1" "Workbench and Table Saw" %}

If you look at the upper-left corner of the image, you'll see that he had a stack of hammers hanging across the top of the pegboard. When I moved in to my current shop, the first thing I built after I mounted the same pegboard to the wall was the hammer rack you see at the top of this page's header image.

In the middle-right of the image, you'll see a rack containing a bunch of baby food jars containing screws, bolts, and other stuff my dad wanted to keep handy. I still have every one of those jars, stored on a wooden shelf as shown in the following image.
