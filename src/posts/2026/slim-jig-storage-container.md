In [Rockler Circle Cutting Jig](/posts/2026/review-circle-cutting-jig/), I explained that even through I liked the jig, I was really disappointed that Rockler built the packaging in such a way that once you took the jig out of the packaging, you couldn't put any of it back. They needed to ship the product in a box, but instead they chose a much less expensive option and simply plastered the parts to a big piece of cardboard.

I own this jig, so I had to figure out how to store it and its associated parts. I decided to build a simple wooden box for it with a magnetic lid. This post shows you how to build something like it.

<div class="text-image">
<div>
<p>Here's an image of the jig pulled out of its packaging. The back contains the only instructions for the product. On the front, behind that little plastic cover shown in the upper-right corner of the image was the jig and associated parts.</p>
Its almost embarrassing, so little attention being paid to how a consumer will store the product after first use. And this from a company that usually delivers solid products.
</div>

<div class="text-image">
<div>
<p>Here's an image of the finished project; a hard case that holds the jig and all the additional parts you need to use it. I have a bunch of long Woodpeckers tools, so I just stack this in the corner with all of them.</p>
</div>
<div>
<div>
<img src="/images/2026/slim-jig-holder-01.jpg" alt="The finished project" class="image fit">
<p class="caption">The finished project</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div class="text-image">
<div>
<p>The shell for the case consists of two plywood or hardboard (like masonite) panels plus two strips of plywood.</p>
<p>For the panels, I used 1/8 inch masonite since I had some lying around; I didn't need anything thicker than that since these panels simply form the structure of the case. I cut the panels wide enough to cover the width of the equipment you're storing in the case plus the thickness of the plywood pieces plus a little more (between 1/4 and 1/2 inches). For panel height, cut the panels a little shorter than the frame pieces you'll cut next. If you're using 1/2 inch plywood for the frame, cut the panel 1/2 inch shorter than the frame. If you're using 3/4 inch plywood for the frame, cut the panel 3/4 inch shorter than the frame.</p>
<p>Cut the plywood pieces to a little thicker than the thickness of the equipment you'll store in the case. In my case, I cut them to about 2 inches.</p>
</div>
<div>
<div>
<img src="/images/2026/slim-jig-holder-02.jpg" alt="Case shell parts" class="image fit">
<p class="caption">Case shell parts</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div class="text-image">
<div>
<p>Next, you need a few pieces of plywood as shown in the image. You'll use the two pieces on the left, we'll call them <strong>Part A</strong> for the top and bottom of the frame. The four pieces on the right, we'll call them <strong>Part B</strong> are used to frame the top and bottom of the case.</p>
<p>Of the two Part A pieces, only one will be used in the final case, the other is a throw-away piece; I'll explain why in a little bit. Cut them to width of the panels minus the thickness (in my example 3/4 inch) of plywood used for the side. </p>
</div>
<div>
<div>
<img src="/images/2026/slim-jig-holder-03.jpg" alt="Additional plywood pieces needed" class="image fit">
<p class="caption">Additional plywood pieces needed</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div class="text-image">
<div>
<p>You probably already do this in your shop, but when it comes to cutting multiple pieces of wood to the same length, I use a stop block on my miter saw like shown in the image. This allows me to setup one piece, then duplicate it. Maybe someday I'll write a post about the extension I built on my miter saw to make this easier.</p>
</div>
<div>
<div>
<img src="/images/2026/slim-jig-holder-04.jpg" alt="Using a stop block to make consistent cuts" class="image fit">
<p class="caption">Using a stop block to make consistent cuts</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div class="text-image">
<div>
<p>Take one of the Part A pieces and clamp it, but <strong>DO NOT GLUE IT</strong> between two of the plywood rails.</p>
<p>This piece sets the open top of the case. Once you glue/tack on the sides later, you'll pull this piece out to leave the top of the case open.</p>
</div>
<div>
<div>
<img src="/images/2026/slim-jig-holder-05.jpg" alt="Starting to build the frame" class="image fit">
<p class="caption">Starting to build the frame</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div class="text-image">
<div>
<p>Next, glue and clamp the other Part A piece at the bottom of the rails as shown in the image. Let the glue cure for at least 30 minutes.</p>
</div>
<div>
<div>
<img src="/images/2026/slim-jig-holder-06.jpg" alt="Gluing the bottom of the frame" class="image fit">
<p class="caption">Gluing the bottom of the frame</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div class="text-image">
<div>
<p>Squeeze some glue on three sides of one of the panels as shown in the image. Squeeze glue on the top of the assembled frame you built in the previous step (also shown in the image). Don't forget to remove the unglued piece of the frame.</p>
<p>Make sure you take into account any extra space you left on each side of the panel (remember, I instructed you to add 1/4 to 1/2 half inch to the width of the panels).</p>
</div>
<div>
<div>
<img src="/images/2026/slim-jig-holder-07.jpg" alt="Gluing the panels to the frame" class="image fit">
<p class="caption">Gluing the panels to the frame</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div class="text-image">
<div>
<p>Center the assembled frame on top of the panel with the glue squeezed on it. When you're done, you should see a little overlap on the panel on either size of the case. Don't worry, we'll take that off later.</p>
</div>
<div>
<div>
<img src="/images/2026/slim-jig-holder-08.jpg" alt="One side of the case assembled" class="image fit">
<p class="caption">One side of the case assembled</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div class="text-image">
<div>
<p>Squeeze some glue on the other panel and the other side of the frame. Then, set the frame on the other panel then clamp it all together.</p>
</div>
<div>
<div>
<img src="/images/2026/slim-jig-holder-09.jpg" alt="Assembling the base case" class="image fit">
<p class="caption">Assembling the base case</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div class="text-image">
<div>
<p>Let the glued pieces sit for at least 30 minutes (or whatever the curing time is for the glue you used) then remove the clamps. The case should something like the image.</p>
</div>
<div>
<div>
<img src="/images/2026/slim-jig-holder-10.jpg" alt="Partially assembled case" class="image fit">
<p class="caption">Partially assembled case</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div class="text-image">
<div>
<p>Use a router with a flush trim bit to strip away the overlapping plywood or hardwood from the frame as shown in the image. I did it this way because I know its almost impossible to cut the frame and panel pieces to they perfectly align. This approach allows me to not care, then make them perfect using the router later. Cool stuff, right?</p>
</div>
<div>
<div>
<img src="/images/2026/slim-jig-holder-11.jpg" alt="Using the router to trim away excess panel" class="image fit">
<p class="caption">Using the router to trim away excess panel</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div class="text-image">
<div>
<p>When you're done, it will look something like this; perfect alignment between the panels and frame.</p>
</div>
<div>
<div>
<img src="/images/2026/slim-jig-holder-12.jpg" alt="Perfectly flush sides" class="image fit">
<p class="caption">Perfectly flush sides</p>
</div>
</div>
