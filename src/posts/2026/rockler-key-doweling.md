## Notes

+ Requires that you create your own backer boards before you can use it
+ A starter set should be included automatically so you can use it out of the box
+ Doesn't include a storage container you can use to store the device without disassembling it first.
+ Material should be more rigid or made out of metal, at least the clamping part

I've always had an interest in different types of joinery and I think that spline joints are particularly interesting and appealing. Several years ago, I purchased Rockler's [Corner Key Doweling Jig](https://www.rockler.com/rockler-corner-key-doweling-jig){target="_blank"} but I never had an opportunity to use it for anything.

---

I recently built a couple of drawers out of 1/2 inch maple plywood using a [rabbet joint](https://www.familyhandyman.com/article/rabbet-joints/){target="_blank"} and I wanted an easy way to reinforce the glued joint. I decided to dig out the corner key doweling jig and give it a whirl.

{% sidebar "Kinda Disappointed" %}
My overall assessment of this jig is that its a great idea and an interesting design, but I'm really disappointed in the implementation. It requires more setup than what I expect from a jig like this and the materials weren't sturdy enough to allow me to consistently cut solid joints using the jig. This is all explained below.
{% endsidebar %}

Basically, this jig allows you to (more or less) accurately drill holes across a corner of a box, drawer, or frame, etc. After you've drilled the hole, you then glue in then cut flush a wooden dowel. This creates a secure cross support for the joint you drilled across. If you're doing this simply to reinforce the joint, it doesn't matter what wood you use for the dowel. If you're doing this for aesthetics, trying to improve the look of the joint, then you use a dowel of a contrasting color or type of wood.

## Basic Operation

After you assemble the jig, more on this later, you basically clamp the jig across the corner and drill your hole. The jig is flexible enough that it allows you to easily configure the distance from the edge and diameter of the drilled hole. 

**Note:** The jig only drills holes at a 45° angle.
